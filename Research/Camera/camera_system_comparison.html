On-mast cabling complexity Higher 4× PoE+ cables from switch box at mast base up to cameras at mast head. PTZ cameras are heavier (2kg each) — bracket and cable management more involved. Lower 4× Cat6 cables, lightweight turret cameras (~0.4kg each). Clean installation. Standard mounting brackets. Cable protection strategy All three systems — same approach All cables run inside the mast structure from camera head down to switch box at base (cameras are at top of mast, switch box is inside a weatherproof enclosure at base). A single armoured or conduit-protected trunk cable then runs underground or in armoured trunking from mast base to the control room/site office. This is the key point you raised — minimal exposed cabling on the site surface. Workers only encounter the mast pole itself.
Risk of cable cutting / damage Low for all systems with correct installation Cables inside mast structure + underground or armoured trunking from mast to office = virtually no accessible cable on the site. The only exposed hardware is the mast itself and camera heads, both of which are at height. This is why the central mast concept is smart — it minimises cable sprawl across a live construction site compared to corner-mounted cameras each needing their own cable run.

07 — Verdict & Recommendation

System A — PTZ Only

Best for sites where a human operator is watching screens and manually directing cameras. The 25× zoom is a genuine forensic tool. However it underserves AI-driven pipelines — variable FOV, B&W nights, and high cost make it a poor primary choice for autonomous detection. Consider it only if your primary use case is active human-monitored security, not automated PPE/headcount analytics.

Recommended for your use case

System C — Hybrid 2+2

Gives your YOLO model stable colour streams 24/7 (ColorVu), while preserving 25× zoom capability for investigation and manual oversight (PTZ). When AI fires an alert, your backend can auto-direct a PTZ to the GPS coordinates of the event for confirmation. Best fault tolerance — a single camera failure doesn't blind the AI. Mid-range cost. Matches your stated use case precisely: AI detection + human response capability.

System B — ColorVu Only

Best value, simplest install, best AI detection performance, best maintainability. If budget is the primary constraint and remote human zoom-in is not required, this is the cleanest solution. The limitation is losing the ability to zoom in on a specific person or vehicle at distance — you rely entirely on what the fixed wide-angle frame shows. For AI-only workflows with no active human operator, this is actually the optimal choice.
